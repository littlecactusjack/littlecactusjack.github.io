# Stage 1 calibration — re-measured on futures

Generated by `python -m futuresres.reporting.calibration_report`. CLAUDE_FUTURES.md §4, §5 Stage 1, §7.

Both numbers the pipeline used were measured on Bitcoin. §4 flags them as inherited and wrong until re-measured; this is that measurement, and it **unblocks Stage 1**.

## A. The bootstrap α

Coverage of the percentile interval on the signed shift `evaluate_signed_signal` computes, block-bootstrapped with the same 240-bar blocks, on **contiguous windows of real returns** — so the tail and the serial dependence are the market's own, not a model's. Under the null the signal is independent of returns, so the true shift is zero and coverage is the fraction of intervals containing it.

| blocks | futures coverage at α=0.05 | ± | crypto | **futures α\*** | crypto α\* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 93.23% | 0.39 | 92.95% | **0.0345** | 0.0312 |
| 100 | 94.00% | 0.39 | 93.40% | **0.0409** | 0.0359 |
| 250 | 94.25% | 0.39 | 94.05% | **0.0411** | 0.04 |
| 500 | 93.89% | 0.39 | 94.75% | **0.0402** | 0.05 |
| 1000 | 94.14% | 0.39 | — | **0.0413** | — |

Pooled across all six (product, horizon) series — 12,000 replications per block count.

### The 115-vs-226 kurtosis gap does not show up in coverage

This was the reason for measuring MNQ and MGC separately, and the answer is that they do not separate:

| blocks | per-cell coverage range | spread | Monte Carlo error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 92.60% – 94.30% | 1.70 pts | ±0.96 pts |
| 100 | 93.20% – 94.60% | 1.40 pts | ±0.96 pts |
| 250 | 93.65% – 94.95% | 1.30 pts | ±0.96 pts |
| 500 | 93.10% – 94.45% | 1.35 pts | ±0.96 pts |
| 1000 | 93.15% – 95.00% | 1.85 pts | ±0.96 pts |

**The spread between cells is the same size as the noise on each.** MNQ at γ₄ = 115 and MGC at γ₄ = 226 produce coverage that cannot be told apart at 2,000 replications, and neither can the 1m, 60m and 180m horizons. A single calibration is therefore what the data supports — six would be fitting noise.

### What to use

Under-coverage is real but **milder than crypto's**: about 1.8 points short at 50 blocks, narrowing to 0.9 at 1000. Note it **never reaches 95%**, so nominal 0.05 is not self-correcting at large samples the way crypto's table implied — a small correction applies at every block count.

## B. The detection floor

Smallest injected effect promoted end-to-end at ≥80%, in units of one bar's volatility at the horizon swept. Noise is a contiguous window of real returns; the edge is a slow regime flipping every ~500 bars, the crypto construction.

| product | horizon | independent obs in 16 years | best measured floor | at n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MNQ | 1m | 2,534,668 | **0.04481×** | 80,000 |
| MNQ | 60m | 39,444 | **0.08446×** | 19,722 |
| MNQ | 180m | 11,768 | **0.3×** | 5,884 |
| MGC | 1m | 3,102,696 | **0.04481×** | 80,000 |
| MGC | 60m | 31,591 | **0.1592×** | 15,795 |
| MGC | 180m | 8,190 | **0.3×** | 2,862 |

### Scaling: crypto's −0.321 is not reproduced

| product | horizon | exponent | points | vs √n (−0.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MNQ | 1m | **-0.686** | 3 | steeper |
| MNQ | 60m | — | 1 | not enough floors |
| MNQ | 180m | — | 1 | not enough floors |
| MGC | 1m | **-0.686** | 3 | steeper |
| MGC | 60m | **-0.613** | 2 | matches |
| MGC | 180m | **+0.000** | 2 | **degenerate** — both rungs gave the same floor |

Crypto measured **-0.321** — markedly shallower than √n, meaning more data bought less than expected. **Futures run −0.61 to −0.69**, and the three real fits agree closely: both 1-minute cells land on exactly −0.686 (each from the same 0.3 → 0.1592 → 0.04481 ladder over 5k/20k/80k) and MGC 60m on −0.613. That is **at or steeper than √n**, so more data helps more here than it did there.

Caveats, in order of size. MGC 180m's +0.000 is not a measurement — both resolving rungs returned the same 0.3× because that is the top of the grid, so there is no slope to fit and it should be read as "no estimate", not as "flat". MNQ 60m and 180m produced only ONE floor each, so no fit is possible at all. And every floor sits on a rung of a 1.885× ladder, carrying roughly half a rung of resolution — which is wide enough that −0.61 and −0.69 are not meaningfully different from each other.

So the honest summary is: **three independent fits, all between −0.6 and −0.7, none consistent with crypto's −0.321.** The direction is settled even though the third decimal is not.

## C. What this means: the constraint has inverted

The crypto catalog closed because effects were **detectable but too small to trade** — everything measured sat under an 8 bps commission floor. Converting these floors to basis points against §4's cost floor asks whether that is still the binding constraint.

| product | horizon | σ per bar | best floor | **floor in bps** | cost floor | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MNQ | 1m | 3.9 bps | 0.04481× | **0.17 bps** | 0.48 bps | 0.4× |
| MNQ | 60m | 30.4 bps | 0.08446× | **2.57 bps** | 0.48 bps | 5.3× |
| MNQ | 180m | 52.2 bps | 0.3× | **15.66 bps** | 0.48 bps | 32.6× |
| MGC | 1m | 3.5 bps | 0.04481× | **0.16 bps** | 0.65 bps | 0.2× |
| MGC | 60m | 26.2 bps | 0.1592× | **4.17 bps** | 0.65 bps | 6.4× |
| MGC | 180m | 47.8 bps | 0.3× | **14.34 bps** | 0.65 bps | 22.1× |

**The constraint inverts WITH HORIZON, and both regimes are present here.**

- **At one minute the floors sit BELOW the cost floor** — 0.17 bps against MNQ's 0.48, 0.16 against MGC's 0.65. This is the crypto situation exactly: the pipeline can establish effects far too small to trade, so COST binds and detection is the easy part.
- **At 60 and 180 minutes the floors sit 5x to 33x ABOVE it** — 2.57 to 15.66 bps on MNQ, 4.17 to 14.34 on MGC. Here the relationship reverses: anything the pipeline can see is comfortably tradeable, and anything too small to see would have paid handsomely. DETECTION binds.

Aggregation is what flips it. Moving from 1m to 180m multiplies the bar's volatility by about 13x while dividing the sample by 180, and the floor responds to sample size far more than the cost does to anything. So the question a hypothesis must answer depends on where it operates: at one minute, *is the effect bigger than half a basis point*; at three hours, *is it bigger than fifteen*.

**Most of this catalog operates at 30 minutes and longer**, which puts it in the detection-bound regime — the opposite of the regime that closed the crypto catalog, and the reason the per-hypothesis check in `reports/detectability.md` matters more than a cost screen would.

### The hard limit at long horizons

Aggregating to 60 or 180 minutes thins the tail — `reports/kurtosis.md` shows γ₄ falling from 115/226 at 1m to 19/14 at 180m — but it destroys sample size, and sample size is what the floor actually responds to:

- **MNQ 60m**: 39,444 independent observations in sixteen years, so the largest testable sample is 19,722 — 1 of 3 rungs resolved a floor.
- **MNQ 180m**: 11,768 independent observations in sixteen years, so the largest testable sample is 5,884 — 1 of 3 rungs resolved a floor.
- **MGC 60m**: 31,591 independent observations in sixteen years, so the largest testable sample is 15,795 — 2 of 3 rungs resolved a floor.
- **MGC 180m**: 8,190 independent observations in sixteen years, so the largest testable sample is 4,095 — 2 of 3 rungs resolved a floor.

**MGC at 180 minutes has the highest floor measured anywhere: 0.3×, about 14 bps.** That is the top rung of the swept ladder — 0.1592× failed to reach 80% and 0.3× reached it — so the floor is bracketed in (0.1592, 0.3]. It is RESOLVED, not unresolved: it is simply high, for the reason the column above gives. 8,190 independent observations is the smallest sample in the study.

The practical consequence is unchanged. A 180-minute MGC hypothesis must clear roughly 14 bps before the pipeline can see it at all, and a null below that carries no information — §7's rule applied to the number this sweep measured.
